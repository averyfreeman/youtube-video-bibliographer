Understanding R-6-12 Zoning Designation
=====================================

The R-6-12 zoning designation describes a low-to-medium density residential district that allows for 6 to 12 housing units per gross acre. This classification, commonly used in Washington state cities like Olympia, balances traditional single-family housing with a wider variety of "missing middle" housing options.

### Permitted Building Densities & Uses

* Density Range: Minimum of 6 and a maximum of 12 units per acre.
* Housing Types: In addition to detached single-family homes, this zone typically permits duplexes, triplexes, courtyard apartments, and townhouses.
* Location: These zones are frequently situated within (1/4) mile of major transit corridors, arterial streets, and employment or neighborhood centers.

### How It Differs from Other Zones

Compared to strictly lower-density zones (like R-4-8, which limits density to 4–8 units per acre), the R-6-12 zone permits higher concentrations of housing and smaller minimum lot sizes to accommodate denser building types while keeping building heights consistent with single-family neighborhoods.

To determine the specific zoning code and regulations for a particular city or county, it is essential to consult the local municipal code, such as the [Olympia Municipal Code](https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/Olympia/html0217/Olympia18/Olympia1804.html). This will provide information on setbacks, building heights, and lot coverage requirements.

### Calculating Units on a Parcel of Land

To calculate the number of units that can be built on a parcel of land under R-6-12 zoning regulations, we need to follow a series of steps.

#### 1. Convert square footage to acres

An acre contains exactly 43,560 square feet. To find the size of a parcel in acres, divide the lot square footage by the square footage of a full acre. For example, an 8,400 square foot parcel would be:
[Parcel Acreage = 8,400 sq ft / 43,560 sq ft/acre ≈ 0.1928 acres]

#### 2. Calculate the minimum density

Multiply the parcel acreage by the zone's minimum requirement of 6 units per acre:
[Minimum Units = 0.1928 acres × 6 units/acre = 1.157 units]
Local municipalities typically round fractional density numbers. A result of 1.157 means you are legally mandated to build at least 1 unit.

#### 3. Calculate the maximum density

Multiply the parcel acreage by the zone's maximum allowance of 12 units per acre:
[Maximum Units = 0.1928 acres × 12 units/acre = 2.314 units]
Because 2.314 fractional units do not equal a whole third unit, the raw mathematical ceiling restricts development to 2 units (such as a duplex or a primary home with an accessory dwelling unit).

#### 4. Check local rounding rules

Local municipal codes handle fractional decimals differently. Some jurisdictions round to the nearest whole number (making 2.314 round down to 2), while others allow you to round up to the next whole number if the fraction exceeds (.50). In rare cases, a city may allow a maximum of 3 units if specific affordable housing or "missing middle" design bonuses apply to your lot.

### Final Answer

On an 8,400 square foot parcel zoned R-6-12, you are legally permitted to build a minimum of 1 unit and a maximum of 2 units.
