## Municipal Grant Programs
If you are looking for municipal grant programs aimed at preventing homelessness or stabilizing tenants, those are administered directly by the government or chosen centralized agencies rather than individual housing providers. The City of Seattle Human Services Department periodically opens grant applications for non-profit providers to distribute rental assistance and legal eviction defense.

## Clarifying Cause IQ Data
The data presented on Cause IQ is not erroneous, but its automated labeling can be highly misleading if you do not know the operational context of the organizations listed. The "Grants made by LIHI" section on Cause IQ lists $0 payouts to three groups: the Asia Pacific Cultural Center, Sound Foundations NW, and House Our Neighbors. However, these are joint-venture operational partnerships, not philanthropic grant awards.

## Tiny Home Villages in Olympia, WA
There are three major tiny home villages currently operating in the Olympia area:
1. Quince Street Village (Downtown Olympia)
	* Status: Operational
	* Capacity: Houses approximately 100 people in small temporary shelters
	* Management: The site was opened by the City of Olympia and is managed 24/7 with on-site support provided by Catholic Community Services
2. Franz Anderson Tiny Home Village (East Olympia)
	* Status: Operational (opened late 2023)
	* Capacity: Consists of 50 tiny homes designed to temporarily transition unhoused adults off local highway rights-of-way
	* Management: Built on land owned by the City of Olympia and operated by Olympia Mutual Aid Partners (OlyMAP), featuring on-site case management, hygiene facilities, and meal programs
3. Quixote Village (West Olympia)
	* Status: Operational permanent-housing model
	* Capacity: Houses 30 individual residents
	* Management: Operated independently by New Horizon Communities

Note that the Low Income Housing Institute (LIHI) previously operated the temporary Plum Street Village in Olympia in partnership with the city, but their localized support has shifted as Thurston County and Olympia city resources transitioned operations to localized providers like OlyMAP and Catholic Community Services.
