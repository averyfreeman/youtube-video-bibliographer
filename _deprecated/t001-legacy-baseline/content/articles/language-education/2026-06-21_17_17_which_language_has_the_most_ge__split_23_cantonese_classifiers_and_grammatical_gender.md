## Cantonese and Grammatical Genders
Cantonese does not have grammatical genders. Instead, it uses a system of classifiers (or measure words) to organize nouns by shape, size, and type. For example, the spoken pronoun "keoi5" means "he," "she," and "it" all at once. To count or point to an object, a specific classifier must be used based on its physical traits.

### Classifiers in Cantonese
Cantonese has various classifiers, including:
- tiu4: used for long, thin objects like snakes, fish, or pants
- zoeng1: used for flat things like paper, tables, or beds
- go3: used for people and many everyday items

Using the correct classifier is crucial in Cantonese, as using an incorrect classifier can be insulting or sound nonsensical. For instance, using tiu4 for a person can be highly insulting or vulgar, while using zoeng1 is grammatically incorrect and sounds confusing.

### Insulting Classifiers
Using tiu4 for a person objectifies them and is used to express anger, contempt, or extreme dismissiveness. However, in casual street slang among close male friends, tiu4 can be used as a rugged, informal way to say "dude" or "bro." Nevertheless, it is still considered vulgar and should not be used in polite company.

### Choosing the Correct Classifier
To avoid accidentally insulting someone, it is essential to choose the correct classifier based on the social setting. The following classifiers are commonly used:
| Classifier | Tone | Best Used For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 位 (wai2) | Highly Respectful | Teachers, customers, doctors, or elders. |
| 個 (go3) | Neutral / Safe | Friends, classmates, or everyday general use. |
| 條 (tiu4) | Vulgar / Disrespectful | Punks, enemies, or highly informal slang. |

By understanding the nuances of Cantonese classifiers, learners can improve their conversational skills and avoid unintentionally offending others.
