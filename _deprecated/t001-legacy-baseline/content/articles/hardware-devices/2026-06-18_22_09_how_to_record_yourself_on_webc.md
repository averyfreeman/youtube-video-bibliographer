## Recording Yourself on Webcam while Screen Recording for Code Examples
Recording yourself on a webcam while screen recording is essential for effective coding tutorials. The most popular and easiest method for developers is using OBS Studio (desktop software) or quick browser-based tools like Loom or Clipchamp to capture your face and screen at the exact same time.

### Dedicated Screen Recorders (Easiest)
These tools do the heavy lifting automatically by combining your webcam feed, microphone, and screen into a single video file.

* Loom: A popular favorite for quick snippets. Simply open the app, choose Screen and Camera, select your IDE or desktop, and hit record.
* Microsoft Clipchamp: Built-in to Windows. Go to the Record & Create tab, select Screen and Camera, grant permissions, and select the coding window you want to share.
* Riverside: A browser-based solution that records your screen and webcam on separate tracks. This is ideal if you want to resize your webcam footage in post-production.

### OBS Studio (Best for Advanced Quality)
OBS is free, open-source software that gives you full control over your layout, which is perfect for maximizing readability in code editors.

* Download and open OBS Studio.
* Under the Sources panel, click the + icon and add Video Capture Device (for your webcam).
* Click the + icon again and add Display Capture (for your screen) or Window Capture (to only record your code editor).
* Drag and resize your webcam feed to a corner of the screen so it doesn't block your code.
* Click Start Recording.

### Zoom / Virtual Meeting (Best for Live Streaming)
If you are recording a lecture or live-coding session, you can use video conferencing tools.

* Start a private meeting.
* Select Share Screen and pick your code editor.
* Turn your webcam on. Most meeting platforms place your webcam video in a small thumbnail over the screen.
* Click Record.

### Tips for Better Code Recording
* Resolution: Make sure to record in at least 1080p so your code text remains legible.
* Editor Zoom: Bump up the font size in your IDE or terminal so viewers do not strain to read it.
* Lighting: Place a light source in front of you (not just behind you) so your face is clearly visible next to your code.
