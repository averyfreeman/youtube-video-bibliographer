Using a Modern TV as a PC Monitor: Considerations and Recommendations
=================================================================

As a potential user of a TV as a PC monitor, it's essential to consider the key factors that affect performance and usability. Modern 60Hz+ TVs can be suitable for this purpose, but the exact use case matters. Standard 60Hz panels are fine for basic office work, while gaming and productivity benefit from true 120Hz or 144Hz panels with HDMI 2.1.

### Advanced Features

Premium TVs often support variable refresh rates like G-SYNC and FreeSync, making gameplay incredibly smooth. With HDMI 2.1, pixel response times are rapid, minimizing motion blur and input lag. Some models, like the LG C5 OLED, deliver incredible contrast and color accuracy for desktop tasks and creative work.

### Key Drawbacks and Trade-Offs

Televisions generally have a lower pixel density (PPI) than dedicated monitors, which can cause text fringing. Small black text on a white background can appear with red or green fringe shadows, leading to eye strain during prolonged reading. Additionally, the physical size of TVs can be a challenge, as they often start at 42-43 inches, requiring a larger desk or wall mounting to avoid feeling like you're sitting in the front row of a movie theater.

### Best Practices for Setup

To get the most out of your TV as a monitor, always use Game Mode or PC Mode to disable image post-processing, which can introduce annoying lag when moving your mouse. Connect your GPU to the TV using a certified HDMI 2.1 cable and enable 4:4:4 Chroma Subsampling for the crispest possible text.
