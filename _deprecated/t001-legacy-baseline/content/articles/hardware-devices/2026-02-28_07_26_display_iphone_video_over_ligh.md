Displaying iPhone Video Over Lightning to HDMI Adapter
=====================================================

To display video from your iPhone on a larger screen, you need to connect a Lightning to HDMI adapter to your device and then link it to your display via an HDMI cable. This setup supports screen mirroring and video output at resolutions up to 1080p HD.

### Connection Steps

To connect your iPhone to a larger screen, follow these steps:

* Plug the adapter into the Lightning port on the bottom of your iPhone.
* Connect an HDMI cable (sold separately) to the HDMI port on the adapter.
* Connect the other end of the HDMI cable to an available HDMI input on your TV, monitor, or projector.
* Connect a power source (optional but recommended) by plugging a Lightning charging cable into the adapter's secondary Lightning port. This keeps your iPhone charged and can improve signal stability.
* Switch the TV input using your remote to match the HDMI port you used.

### Recommended Adapters

When choosing a Lightning to HDMI adapter, consider the following options:

* **Apple Lightning Digital AV Adapter**: Supports up to 1080p HD and is highly rated for compatibility by users on Apple Support. Official versions typically support streaming apps like YouTube and Hulu, unlike some uncertified alternatives.
* **4XEM Lightning to HDMI Adapter**: Features reinforced molded ends for enhanced durability compared to standard cables. This is an MFi Certified option, ensuring reliable performance with Apple devices.

### Troubleshooting Common Issues

If you encounter issues with your connection, try the following:

* **No Signal**: Ensure all cables are firmly plugged in. Try disconnecting and reconnecting the adapter.
* **App Compatibility (DRM)**: Some streaming apps (e.g., Netflix, Prime Video) may block mirroring due to HDCP copyright protection, especially on uncertified third-party adapters.
* **Update Required**: If the adapter is not recognized, ensure your iPhone is running the latest iOS version. Some adapters may require a brief wait for an automatic firmware/driver update once connected.
* **Cleaning**: Check the iPhone’s Lightning port for dust or debris that might prevent a solid connection.

### Resolving a Pitch-Black Screen

If your monitor's screen is pitch black while your iPhone is connected to it via HDMI, try the following targeted steps:

1. **The "Power-On Sequence" Fix**: HDMI devices often fail to "handshake" if connected in the wrong order. This reset often clears the black screen:
	* Unplug everything: iPhone, adapter, and HDMI cable from the monitor.
	* Power cycle the monitor: Turn the monitor off and unplug it from its wall outlet for 30 seconds.
	* Replug in this order:
		+ Plug the monitor back into power and turn it on.
		+ Connect the HDMI cable to the monitor first, then the adapter.
		+ Connect the adapter to your iPhone.
2. **Verify Power Delivery**: Most Lightning to HDMI adapters require external power to push a video signal to a monitor. Ensure a Lightning charging cable is plugged into the port on the side of the adapter and connected to a wall outlet (not just a computer USB port, which may provide insufficient voltage).
3. **Check for HDCP/DRM Restrictions**: If you see a black screen only when trying to play video from apps like Netflix, Hulu, or Disney+, it is likely a copyright protection (HDCP) issue. Test with the Home Screen: If you can see your iPhone's home screen but the screen goes black when you hit "Play" in an app, the content is being blocked by the adapter's lack of HDCP compliance. Use a genuine Apple Digital AV Adapter or an MFi-certified alternative that supports HDCP.
4. **Hardware Basics**:
	* **Monitor Input**: Use the monitor's physical buttons to manually select the correct HDMI Input (e.g., HDMI 1 vs HDMI 2).
	* **Clean the Port**: Use a toothpick or compressed air to gently remove lint from your iPhone's Lightning port; even a tiny amount can let the phone charge but block high-speed video data.
	* **Try a Different Cable**: If possible, test with a different HDMI cable to rule out a faulty cord.
