## The Potential Role of Propylene Glycol
Propylene glycol, a common ingredient in foods, cosmetics, and medications, can also trigger or worsen cuts in the corners of the mouth due to its potential as a skin irritant and allergen.

### How Propylene Glycol Causes Lip Cuts
Propylene glycol can cause:
* **Allergic Contact Cheilitis**: An allergic reaction or hypersensitivity on the lips, leading to inflammation, redness, and peeling.
* **Irritant Contact Dermatitis**: Direct skin irritation, disrupting the protective skin barrier in the delicate skin of the mouth corners.
* **Perioral Dermatitis**: A red, burning rash around the mouth that can spread to the corners, mimicking or compounding angular cheilitis.

### Common Sources of Propylene Glycol
Check the labels of products that frequently touch the lips or mouth for propylene glycol, including:
* Oral care products (toothpastes, mouthwashes, dental gels)
* Lip products (lip balms, lipsticks, glosses)
* Skincare products (face washes, moisturizers, sunscreens)
* Supplements and medications (liquid vitamins, capsule coatings, flavorings in powdered supplements)

### Next Steps for Identification and Relief
* **Check Your Labels**: Inspect the ingredient list of your potassium bicarbonate supplement and toothpaste for "Propylene Glycol" or "PG".
* **Eliminate and Simplify**: Switch to a 100% pure ingredient like pure white petroleum jelly (Vaseline) for lip care.
* **Switch Toothpastes**: Use a natural or basic toothpaste formulated without propylene glycol or sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS).
* **See a Dermatologist**: If the cracks persist, a dermatologist can perform a patch test to confirm if propylene glycol or another ingredient is the cause of your symptoms.

To help narrow down the culprit, consider whether you are using a flavored powder or liquid supplement and if you have recently changed your brand of toothpaste or lip balm.
