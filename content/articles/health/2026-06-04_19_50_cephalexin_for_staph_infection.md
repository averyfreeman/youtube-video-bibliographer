# Persistent Infection After Cephalexin Treatment
If a bacterial infection around the mouth persists after completing a full course of cephalexin, it is essential to seek immediate medical attention. The infection may be caused by a resistant bacterial strain or an underlying medical issue that requires urgent professional re-evaluation to prevent the infection from spreading deeper or into the bloodstream.

## Why the Infection Might Still Be Present
There are several reasons why the infection may not have been fully cleared by the cephalexin treatment:
* **MRSA Resistance**: Cephalexin is ineffective against Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), a common cause of persistent staph infections.
* **Different Pathogen**: The infection may be caused by a virus (like herpes simplex, which causes cold sores) or a fungus (like Candida, which causes angular cheilitis), neither of which respond to antibiotics.
* **Underlying Conditions**: Conditions like eczema, severe acne, or perioral dermatitis can mimic the appearance of a staph infection or make the skin highly vulnerable to recurrent bacterial overgrowth.
* **Inadequate Course**: If any doses were missed, or if the duration prescribed was too short for the severity of the infection, the bacteria may not have been fully eradicated.

## Next Steps with a Doctor
When seeking medical attention, the doctor will likely:
* **Perform a Wound Culture**: Swab the area around the mouth to identify the exact bacteria present and test which antibiotics will successfully kill it.
* **Prescribe Alternative Antibiotics**: If MRSA is suspected or confirmed, they may switch the medication to oral antibiotics like trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim), doxycycline, or clindamycin.
* **Prescribe Topical Treatments**: For localized superficial infections, they might combine or replace oral medications with a prescription-strength topical antibiotic ointment like mupirocin.

## Immediate Self-Care and Safety Steps
To manage the infection and prevent further complications:
* **Do Not Self-Medicate**: Never take leftover antibiotics or borrow someone else's medication, as taking the wrong antibiotic can worsen resistance and delay proper treatment.
* **Avoid Touching the Area**: Do not pick, squeeze, or scratch the sores around the mouth, which can push the bacteria deeper into the tissue or spread it to other parts of the face.
* **Practice Strict Hygiene**: Wash hands frequently with soap and water, use a separate towel for the face, and change the pillowcase daily to prevent re-infecting oneself.

To determine the best course of action, it is crucial to provide the doctor with detailed information about the infection, including the number of days since completing the cephalexin treatment and any new or worsening symptoms, such as fever, increased swelling, or spreading redness.
