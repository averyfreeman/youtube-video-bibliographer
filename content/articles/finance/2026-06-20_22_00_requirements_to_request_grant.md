# Affordable Housing Options with the Low Income Housing Institute (LIHI)
The Low Income Housing Institute (LIHI) offers affordable apartments and Tiny Houses, but there is no centralized application process. To check for vacancies or waitlist openings, it's necessary to contact the property managers of individual LIHI locations directly.

## Requirements to Lease a LIHI Apartment
To be eligible to lease a LIHI apartment, the following requirements must be met:
* Income Limits: Total household income must fall within specific percentages of the Area Median Income (AMI), typically at or below 30% to 80%.
* Minimum Income: For fixed-rent LIHI units, gross monthly income must equal 2 to 2.5 times the monthly rent.
* Application & Fees: Every household member 18 or older must submit an application and a non-refundable screening fee (usually around $56).
* Screening Criteria: Background checks covering rental history, credit history, and criminal history must be passed.
* Required Documentation: If selected for an interview, a valid government-issued ID, Social Security card, proof of income (such as recent pay stubs), and bank statements must be provided.

## Checking Property Listings and Availability
To check which properties have open waitlists or available vacancies, the LIHI Property Listings can be viewed.

## Understanding LIHI's Funding Model
LIHI is a non-profit developer and operator that receives and applies for public grants, corporate sponsorships, and philanthropic funding to build affordable housing and manage tiny house villages. They do not award grants or act as a grant-making foundation.

## Grant Criteria and Partnerships
When money is distributed for housing projects, government entities award the funds to LIHI. For example:
* City and County Funding: The City of Seattle Office of Housing and King County issue Requests for Proposals (RFPs) for non-congregate shelters and affordable housing. LIHI must compete for these awards by proving they have an eligible site, cost reasonableness, and a plan for long-term operational stability.
* Capital Grants: LIHI secures funding through streams like the Washington State Housing Trust Fund or the federal Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) program to build their properties.

LIHI frequently partners with other local community-based organizations, behavioral health providers, and vocational programs to operate their sites. Rather than issuing grants, LIHI enters into vendor contracts, professional service agreements, or operational partnerships. Entities wishing to work with LIHI on a project must usually demonstrate:
* Active non-profit status in good standing with the Washington Secretary of State.
* Specialized capacity to deliver services like case management, mental health support, or substance use counseling.
* Alignment with localized Area Median Income (AMI) targets (typically serving individuals making 30% to 50% of the AMI).
