Using a 0% Introductory Balance Transfer Rate to Pay Off a Car Loan
=================================================================

As a financial planner, I have evaluated the current credit market to find strategies that can help eliminate interest on car loans. One powerful approach is transferring an auto loan to a 0% APR credit card. This method allows you to move high-interest auto debt to an interest-free period, typically lasting 12 to 21 months.

### How to Execute the Transfer

There are several ways to execute the transfer, depending on your credit card issuer's policies:

*   **Direct Transfer**: Some issuers, like Citi or Discover, allow you to provide your auto loan account number and lender details to pay the balance directly.
*   **Balance Transfer Checks**: Many cards provide paper "convenience checks" that you can write to your auto lender or to yourself to deposit and then pay the loan.
*   **Third-Party Services**: If your issuer doesn't support direct loan transfers, you can use services like Plastiq to pay your lender with a credit card for a fee (usually around 2.9%).

### Key Benefits

The benefits of using a 0% introductory balance transfer rate to pay off a car loan include:

*   **Interest Savings**: You can avoid all interest charges for the duration of the introductory period if you pay the balance in full.
*   **Early Title Release**: Because the credit card payment technically "pays off" the auto loan, you may receive your car title sooner.
*   **Unsecured Debt**: Transferring the debt moves the collateral away from your car; if you default on the card, the vehicle cannot be repossessed.

### Critical Risks & Costs

However, there are also critical risks and costs to consider:

*   **Transfer Fees**: Most cards charge a 3% to 5% fee on the amount transferred, which might outweigh your potential interest savings.
*   **Standard APR Trap**: If you don't pay off the balance before the intro period ends, the remaining amount will be subject to the card's standard APR, which is often 20% or higher—significantly more than a typical car loan.
*   **Credit Score Impact**: Placing a large loan on a credit card can drastically increase your credit utilization ratio, potentially causing a temporary drop in your credit score.
*   **Credit Limits**: You might not be granted a high enough credit limit to cover the entire car loan balance.

### When It Makes Sense

This strategy makes sense when:

*   You have a relatively low balance remaining on the car.
*   You are certain you can repay the full amount within the 0% APR window.
*   The 3-5% transfer fee is less than the interest you would have paid over the same period.

### Top Recommendation: Wells Fargo Reflect Card

The Wells Fargo Reflect Card is currently the strongest option for this strategy, offering one of the industry's longest introductory windows:

*   **Promotional Period**: 21 months of 0% intro APR on qualifying balance transfers.
*   **Moratorium on Interest**: No interest is charged on the transferred balance during these 21 months, provided you make at least the minimum monthly payments.
*   **Auto Loan Acceptance**: Unlike some issuers that only allow credit-to-credit transfers, Wells Fargo explicitly allows balance transfers for auto loans and other installment debt from different lenders.
*   **Transfer Mechanism**: You can initiate the transfer during the application or afterward via Wells Fargo Online.

### Alternative for Lower Fees: Citi Diamond Preferred Card

The Citi Diamond Preferred Card also offers 21 months at 0% APR, with a lower 3% intro fee if you transfer within the first 4 months (5% thereafter). However, Citi is sometimes more restrictive regarding non-credit card debt; they often require using "convenience checks" sent in the mail to pay off a car loan rather than a direct electronic transfer.

### Impact on Insurance Premiums

Paying off your loan and receiving your title from the credit union fundamentally changes your insurance requirements:

*   **Elimination of Mandatory Coverage**: Lenders typically require Comprehensive (theft, fire, nature) and Collision (accidents) coverage to protect their collateral. Once the lien is cleared, you are legally only required to carry your state's minimum Liability coverage.
*   **Potential Savings**: Dropping comprehensive and collision can reduce your premium by 30% to 50%, depending on your car's value and location.

### Strategic Caution

As your advisor, I recommend only dropping these coverages if your vehicle is older or its total value is less than 10 times the cost of the annual premium. If the car is still high-value, you might consider increasing your deductible instead of dropping coverage entirely; this lowers premiums while maintaining a safety net for the large asset you just moved onto your credit card.
