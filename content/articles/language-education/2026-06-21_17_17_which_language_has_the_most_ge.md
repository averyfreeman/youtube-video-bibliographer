# Introduction to Grammatical Genders in Languages
When discussing the concept of "most genders" in languages, linguists typically refer to grammatical gender, which is the categorization of nouns based on their agreement with other words in a sentence. This concept is distinct from social gender identities. The languages with the highest number of grammatical genders are primarily found in the Niger-Congo and Caucasian language families.

## Languages with the Highest Number of Grammatical Genders
The Niger-Congo languages, particularly those within the Bantu family, hold the record for the highest number of grammatical genders. Languages such as Fula, spoken in West Africa, and Bantu languages like Swahili, Zulu, and Xhosa, have between 10 and 20 noun classes. In these languages, nouns are categorized into distinct groups, including humans, animals, plants, tools, liquids, and abstract qualities. Every adjective, pronoun, and verb associated with a noun must match its specific class.

## Other Language Families with Elaborate Gender Systems
Other language isolates and families also feature complex gender systems. For example, languages in the Caucasian family, such as Chechen and Batsbi, possess exceptionally complex systems, with Chechen having around 6 gender classes.

## Traditional Indo-European Languages and Gender-Neutral Languages
In contrast, most traditional Indo-European languages, such as Spanish, French, and German, generally feature 2 to 3 genders (Masculine, Feminine, and Neuter). On the other hand, many major languages, including Finnish, Hungarian, and Mandarin Chinese, are completely gender-neutral, utilizing no grammatical gender at all for nouns.
