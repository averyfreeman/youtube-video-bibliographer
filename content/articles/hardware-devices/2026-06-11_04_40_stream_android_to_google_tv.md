Streaming Android to Google TV
==============================

To stream content from an Android phone to a Google TV, both devices must be connected to the same Wi-Fi network. There are two primary methods for streaming: casting specific apps and mirroring the entire screen.

### Method 1: Casting Specific Apps

For video and audio apps, such as YouTube or Netflix, mirroring the entire screen is not necessary. To cast content from these apps:

* Ensure the Android phone and Google TV are on the same Wi-Fi network.
* Open the streaming app on the Android phone and select the desired content.
* Look for the Cast icon (a rectangle with a Wi-Fi symbol) within the app.
* Select the Google TV from the list of available devices. The content will play directly on the TV.

### Method 2: Screen Mirroring via Quick Settings

To duplicate the entire phone display on the TV, which is useful for viewing photos or browsing:

* Swipe down twice from the top of the Android screen to open the Quick Settings panel.
* Locate the Cast, Screen Cast, Smart View, or Wireless Projection button.
* Tap the icon and select the Google TV from the available devices.
* Confirm by tapping Start Now. To disconnect, swipe down again and tap Disconnect.

### Method 3: Screen Mirroring via the Google Home App

If the Android phone does not have a native screen cast button in the Quick Settings menu, the Google Home app can be used:

* Download and open the Google Home app from the Play Store.
* Ensure the app is signed in and connected to the same Wi-Fi network as the TV.
* Tap the Devices tab and select the Google TV.
* Tap Cast my screen at the bottom, followed by Cast screen, and then confirm with Start Now.

Cooking Pork Ribs
================

A 1-pound rack of pork ribs will take 45 to 60 minutes to cook completely at 400°F (204°C). To achieve optimal results, follow these guidelines:

### 1. Check Internal Temperature

* Food Safety: Cook until the internal temperature reaches a minimum of 145°F (63°C), followed by a 3-minute rest.
* Rib Tenderness: For optimal tenderness, where the meat easily pulls away from the bone, aim for an internal temperature between 195°F (91°C) and 205°F (96°C).

### 2. Follow Best Practices

* Use Foil: Wrap the ribs tightly in aluminum foil for the first 30–40 minutes to trap moisture and prevent the small 1 lb rack from drying out.
* Uncover to Crisp: Remove the foil for the final 15–20 minutes of cooking to let the exterior brown or to caramelize any barbecue sauce applied.
* Avoid Bone: When checking the temperature with a meat thermometer, insert it into the thickest part of the meat without touching the bone, as bones register heat incorrectly.

A 1-pound rack of pork ribs needs to cook at 400°F (204°C) for 45 to 60 minutes until it reaches an internal temperature of at least 145°F (63°C) for safety, or up to 205°F (96°C) for maximum tenderness. For a tailored recipe, consider the type of ribs (Baby Back or St Louis style), the use of a dry rub or wet sauce, and the preferred cooking method (oven, air fryer, or grill).
